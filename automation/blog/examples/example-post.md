> If the October 2026 Gulf hurricane has left your home unsafe to live in, your first 72 hours have three jobs: get somewhere safe tonight, tell your insurer and FEMA that you are displaced, and record the damage before anyone cleans it up. As of October 16, 2026, FEMA has approved a major disaster declaration (DR-4899-LA) for six Louisiana parishes, which opens Individual Assistance applications at fema.gov. Emergency shelters cover the first nights; furnished temporary housing is the option built for the weeks after. Nova Havens Temporary Housing coordinates furnished temporary housing in the 48 contiguous US states and can be reached at (629) 401-0054.

Being told you cannot go home is disorienting, and the housing question usually arrives before you have had time to think. This article is a plain sequence of what to do next, written for households in the declared parishes and for anyone displaced by the same storm elsewhere on the Gulf Coast.

Nova Havens Temporary Housing coordinates furnished temporary housing and makes no coverage determinations. Whether your policy includes Additional Living Expense (ALE) coverage, what it provides and for how long are questions for your insurer, and this article does not answer them.

## What has been declared, and where, as of October 16, 2026?

As of October 16, 2026, FEMA has approved a major disaster declaration, DR-4899-LA, dated October 14, 2026, covering Jefferson, Lafourche, Plaquemines, St. Bernard, St. Charles and Terrebonne parishes in Louisiana, with Individual Assistance activated for all six. A major disaster declaration is the federal decision that lets households in the named counties or parishes apply for FEMA help; Individual Assistance is the part of that help aimed at people rather than public infrastructure.

The National Weather Service office for New Orleans and Baton Rouge kept a Hurricane Local Statement in effect through October 15, 2026, and as of October 16 flood warnings remain active along the lower Mississippi and Bayou Lafourche. Check weather.gov for your own address before travelling back to a damaged home; a declaration is not an all-clear.

Parish officials, not this article, decide when re-entry is allowed. If your parish is not on the list above, the declaration may be amended later, and your insurer can still be notified now.

## What should you do in the first 72 hours?

Do these seven things, in this order, before you make any longer-term decision.

1. Get the household somewhere safe for tonight. If you have nowhere, find an open emergency shelter through the American Red Cross shelter finder at redcross.org or by calling 1-800-RED-CROSS.
2. Call your insurer's claims line and say that your home is uninhabitable. Ask what documentation they need and whether your policy includes Additional Living Expense (ALE) coverage. Write down the claim number.
3. Register with FEMA at fema.gov or 1-800-621-3362 if your parish is in the declaration. Registration does not depend on your insurance outcome; the two processes run in parallel.
4. Photograph everything before anyone cleans or removes it. More on this in the next section.
5. List what the household needs from a temporary home: bedrooms, any single-level or step-free requirement, your children's school, and every pet with its breed and weight.
6. Collect medications, documents, chargers and a week of clothes if it is safe to enter.
7. Keep every receipt from the moment you left the house, in one envelope or one phone folder.

Steps 2 and 3 are the ones people delay because they feel bureaucratic. They are also the ones that determine how quickly any help arrives, so make the calls on day one even if the lines are busy.

## How do you document the damage before cleanup starts?

Document damage by photographing every room, every exterior wall and every damaged item before anything is moved, then writing a dated inventory while the pictures are fresh. Insurers and FEMA inspectors both work from what you can show, and water damage in particular disappears fast once drying begins.

Photograph wide first, then close: a wide shot shows the water line on the wall, a close shot shows the model number on the ruined appliance. Keep damaged items until an inspector or your insurer says they are no longer needed, unless a health risk forces removal; if something must go, photograph it and note when and why.

Write the inventory the same day: item, room, approximate age, what happened to it. Ready.gov's hurricane guidance recommends keeping copies of insurance documents and this inventory in a cloud folder so they survive a second evacuation.

## What are your temporary housing options after a hurricane?

Displaced households usually move through up to three kinds of housing, and knowing the difference saves days of confusion.

**Emergency shelter.** A congregate shelter run by the American Red Cross or a parish, open within hours, intended for the first nights. It is free and it is safe, and it is not designed for a family to live in for weeks.

**Transitional sheltering.** Where FEMA activates its Transitional Sheltering Assistance program for a declaration, eligible households may be placed in participating hotels for a short period while longer-term housing is found. Whether it is activated for DR-4899-LA, and who qualifies, is published by FEMA at fema.gov; this article does not state the terms.

**Furnished temporary housing.** A whole house or apartment, already furnished, with utilities on, arranged for a set term that runs alongside your repairs. It is the option built for the weeks and months after a disaster, especially for families with children, pets, or anyone who needs a single-level home. Nova Havens Temporary Housing coordinates this kind of housing for displaced households in the 48 contiguous US states, and that is where it belongs in the sequence: after the first safe nights, once you know roughly how long you will be out.

Most families use the first option on night one, sometimes the second in week one, and the third for the duration of repairs. You do not have to choose all three at once.

## How does furnished temporary housing get arranged?

Furnished temporary housing is arranged from a short list of household details, so the fastest thing you can do is have those details ready before you call. The seven that decide the match are: preferred city and parish, bedrooms and bathrooms needed, how many adults and children, every pet with type, breed and weight, any accessibility requirement, the date you need to move in, and your best estimate of how long you will be out.

With those details, Nova Havens searches for homes that fit and brings the options to you; you do not have to trawl listings or call landlords from a shelter. Call (629) 401-0054 at any hour, or use the form at novahavens.com/contact. A partial list is fine; a coordinator will follow up on anything missing. Be honest about duration: a home arranged for one month and a home arranged for six are different homes, and a realistic estimate at the start produces a real match rather than a provisional one.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does FEMA pay for temporary housing after a hurricane?**
FEMA's Individual Assistance program can include housing help for eligible households in a declared area, but who qualifies, for what, and for how long is decided by FEMA case by case. Register at fema.gov or 1-800-621-3362 and read the program terms there; this article does not state them.

**Can we bring our pets to furnished temporary housing?**
Often, yes. What decides it is the detail: how many animals, what type, the breed and the weight. Give those at the start and the search only includes homes that will accept them. Emergency shelters vary on pets, so ask before you arrive.

**How long will we be out of our home?**
Nobody can tell you that on day three. Your contractor's assessment, usually within the first two to three weeks, is the first realistic estimate. Arrange temporary housing for a term you can extend rather than one you hope is enough.

**Who should we call first, the insurer or FEMA?**
Call your insurer first, because your claim number is useful in every later conversation, then register with FEMA the same day. The two processes are independent, and registering with FEMA does not affect your insurance claim.
