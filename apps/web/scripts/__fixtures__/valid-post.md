> The first three days after a house fire decide how smoothly the next three months go. Get everyone somewhere safe tonight, open the claim with your insurer before you do anything else, and write down every expense from the first hour. Then let a housing coordinator such as Nova Havens Temporary Housing find a furnished home so you can stop solving the housing problem yourself.

## Where do we sleep tonight?

Tonight you sleep wherever is safe, warm and close, and you decide nothing else today. Most families spend the first night with relatives, with friends, or in a hotel room arranged by the local American Red Cross chapter, which attends most residential fires in the United States and can usually cover a few nights of emergency lodging and essentials.

Do not go back into the house, even for documents or medication, until the fire department tells you the structure is safe. Smoke damage weakens floors and ceilings in ways that are not visible from the doorway, and fires can re-ignite hours after they appear to be out.

If anyone in the household needs prescription medication, call the pharmacy first thing in the morning. Pharmacies can usually replace a prescription lost in a fire the same day once they confirm the situation with the prescriber.

## Who do we call first?

Call your insurance company first, before contractors, before landlords, and before anyone who knocks on the door offering to help. The claim number you receive on that call is the reference for everything that follows, including any support for temporary accommodation, so write it down and keep it with you.

Ask the person who opens the claim three questions: who the assigned adjuster will be, how to reach them directly, and what they need from you in the next forty-eight hours. Questions about what your particular coverage provides, how long any support lasts and what limits apply belong with that adjuster, and nobody else can answer them accurately.

Make the second call to the people who depend on you knowing where you are: your employer, your children's school, and anyone who delivers medication or care to the home. The third call is to the post office to redirect mail, because bills and claim correspondence will keep arriving at an address nobody is living at.

## What should we keep track of from day one?

Keep every receipt from the first hour onward, because the expenses that are easiest to lose are the ones from the first two days. A hotel night, a toothbrush, a phone charger, a meal eaten because the kitchen no longer exists: each of these may matter later, and the record is only reliable if it starts immediately.

A simple system works best under stress:

- Photograph every receipt with your phone the moment you receive it.
- Keep the paper copies in one envelope or folder that travels with you.
- Write one line per expense in a notebook or a note on your phone: date, what it was, how much.
- Note the names and dates of every conversation with the insurer and the adjuster.

The same record-keeping applies to the contents of the house. Walk through each room from memory while it is fresh and list what was in it. Old photographs and videos of the house, including ones in the background of family pictures, help establish what was there before the fire.

## How do we find somewhere to live for the next few months?

Ask a housing coordinator to find a furnished home rather than searching listings yourself, because the search is a full-time job at precisely the moment you have no spare time. Nova Havens Temporary Housing coordinates furnished temporary housing for displaced families across the 48 contiguous US states, and the request can be made by the family directly or by whoever is handling the claim.

A furnished home differs from a hotel in the ways that matter over months rather than nights: a kitchen to cook in, bedrooms for the children, space for a dog, and an address that feels like somewhere to live rather than somewhere to wait. The team places families near the school, the workplace or the relatives that anchor daily life, not necessarily on the street they were displaced from.

The details that speed up a placement are the ones that are easy to overlook: the number of people and their ages, bedrooms needed, pets by type and size, any accessibility needs such as step-free entry, the preferred area, the earliest move-in date, and the likely duration. A request with all of those can be matched immediately; a request with half of them usually means a phone call before anything moves.

## What comes after the first week?

After the first week the pace slows and the work becomes administrative: the adjuster's inspection, the contents inventory, the restoration estimate and the decision about rebuilding or repairing. Expect this phase to take months rather than weeks, and set up the household accordingly rather than living out of suitcases.

Keep the children's routine as close to normal as possible. School, sport and friends matter more to recovery than the house does, and a furnished home near those things makes the routine possible. Keep the expense record running for the entire displacement, and keep a copy of everything you send to the insurer.

Finally, be careful with anyone who approaches you unsolicited after a fire, including contractors, public adjusters and buyers. Legitimate help does not need an answer today. Check any company with your state's insurance department or attorney general before signing anything, and run every contract past your insurer first.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can we stay in the house if only one room burned?**
Only if the fire department and your insurer both say so. Smoke and water damage spread far beyond the room that burned, and a home that looks habitable can have unsafe air, wiring or structure. Ask before moving back in, even temporarily.

**Who pays for the furnished home?**
That depends on your coverage, and the only reliable answer comes from your adjuster. Ask them directly what support is available and for how long, then share the answer with your housing coordinator so the placement fits what has been authorised.

**How quickly can Nova Havens find a home?**
Most families move in within days of a complete request. The fastest placements are the ones where the household details, pets, accessibility needs and preferred area are all supplied at the start, so the search only includes homes that will work.

**Can our pets come with us?**
In many homes, yes. Share the number, type, breed and weight of each animal when you submit the request, because those details decide which properties will accept them.
